Design the edit

Paste the locus and the repair oligo — plain sequence, FASTA or a GenBank record. Coding bases are the uppercase ones; a GenBank CDS feature sets the case for you. The predicted HDR allele is rebuilt from the oligo's own homology arms, so any silent recoding you designed in comes along with it.

Loaded with a synthetic example: a C-terminal 3×FLAG knock-in with a GSGS linker and one silent PAM mutation. Not real sequence — replace it with yours.

Check your clones

Drop in Sanger reads as FASTA. Each trace is oriented, its ragged ends clipped, and aligned to the predicted allele — and to the unedited gene, so a wild-type clone is called as one rather than as a big deletion.

3

Alignment

The predicted allele on top, clones below. Homology arms, insert and designed recoding are marked on the reference; only differences are drawn on the reads unless you ask for every base. Where a clone carries extra bases, the reference opens a gap so you can read straight down and see exactly what was inserted.

Nothing aligned yetBuild an edit and paste some reads.
Predicted allele = the oligo replacing the genomic stretch its arms span, which is the HDR outcome when both arms cross over. Reads are placed by 11-mer seeding and a local affine alignment (match +2 / mismatch −4 / gap −10,−1); N scores 0, so a noisy leading stretch neither matches nor penalises. Status calls are heuristics from a single consensus sequence — a heterozygote's double peaks live in the trace, not in the FASTA, so confirm mixed clones on the chromatogram.
